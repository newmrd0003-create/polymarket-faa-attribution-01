Forecast Polymarket's FAA outage attribution prices minute by minute, 4 hours ahead

## The task
Forecast the Yes price of every trading market of Polymarket's FAA outage attribution family at every minute of the 4 hours after each checkpoint. The final has twelve checkpoints, 4 hours apart, from the deadline to 44 hours after it, so its cases together cover every minute of the 48 hours from the deadline to 48 hours after it. Your program, `node /bot/main.ts` (Node 22) or `python3 /bot/main.py` (Python 3.13) at the root of your repository, is run once per case: it reads that case's file at /input, the prices up to its checkpoint, and writes one forecast file to /output: for each market and each of the 240 minutes after the checkpoint, five quantiles of that minute's price. It runs in a sandbox with no network, so it cannot read Polymarket: it forecasts. Your score is the mean pinball loss over every case, market, minute and quantile (Scoring).

## The market and its prices
- The family is four Polymarket events, each one Yes/No market: whether the U.S. government officially attributes the November 13, 2026 FAA outage to Iran (event 1382231, slug faa-outage-attributed-to-iran), to a foreign actor (1382331, faa-outage-attributed-to-a-foreign-actor), to a hack (1382398, faa-outage-attributed-to-hack), or to an AI sandbox escape (1382436, faa-outage-attributed-to-an-ai-sandbox-escape). Each event's own rules, on its Polymarket page, say what counts as an attribution to it. Each market's Yes price, from 0 to 1, is the crowd's chance of that attribution.
- The universe is the family's 4 markets that were trading when the contest's universe was frozen, 2026-11-14T00:01:48Z: active, not closed, accepting orders and traded at least once, in the events' order. Each is named by its conditionId (`id`), its name, its slug and its Yes token, and each is one of the four events above. A market, or a sibling event, Polymarket adds later is not in it.
- A market's price is its Yes token's price as Polymarket's public price history gives it, GET https://clob.polymarket.com/prices-history?market=<yes token>&startTs=<s>&endTs=<s>&fidelity=1: about one point a minute, each the midpoint of the Yes order book (0.295 on a book quoting 0.29 and 0.30), unrounded. The house puts it on a 1-minute UTC grid: the price at minute m (its first second) is the last point at or before m, carried forward through minutes with no point of their own. A market's grid starts at the first whole minute at or after its first point. Every price you are given, and every price you are scored against, is on that grid.
- Prices move in steps: often not at all for minutes, then a tick, and now and then a jump. In the family's first minutes of trading, the Iran market went from 0.49 to 0.275 in the minute to 2026-11-13T21:42:00Z, and the AI sandbox escape market from 0.215 to 0.02 in the minute to 2026-11-13T22:38:00Z.

## The cases
- A case is a checkpoint `at` and the 240 minutes after it, at+1 to at+240. The final's twelve cases are h00 to h11, h00 at the deadline, and each next one 4 hours later, h11 44 hours after the deadline. The house records the prices while they happen, so no one, the house included, has a case's answer before the case's minutes have passed.
- A case holds every universe market that had a price by its checkpoint (in the final, all 4), each with its prices from the 72 hours up to and including the checkpoint (4,320 minutes), or from its grid's first minute when that is later. Nothing after the checkpoint is in it: a case is causal, and each run sees its own case only.
- The practice is six cases, p00 to p05, 4 hours apart from 2026-11-12T06:00:00Z to 2026-11-13T02:00:00Z, answered through 2026-11-13T06:00:00Z. It is cut from Polymarket's "Next CEO of NVIDIA?" event, another event of the same shape, because the FAA markets were created on 2026-11-13 and hold no history to cut a practice from: its markets are that event's, not the final's, and its score is no guide to the final. The markets started at different times, so p00, p01 and p02 hold 19 markets, p03 holds 23 and p04 and p05 all 24; a case lists the markets it scores.

## Your input
- /input/<id>.in, the one case your run is given (h00, say, or p03): UTF-8 JSON, keys sorted, no whitespace, one object: `schema` ("makefun/polymarket-case/v1"), `competition`, `case` (its id), `at` (its checkpoint, ISO 8601 UTC with milliseconds, a whole minute), `horizon_minutes` (240), `quantiles` ([0.05,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.95]), `event` (for the final, `{"slug":"faa-outage-attribution","title":"FAA outage attribution markets"}`, the family's own label, not a tradeable event; for the practice, the NVIDIA event's) and `markets`: a list, each `id` (its conditionId), `name`, `slug`, `yes_token`, `start` (the minute of the first price in its `prices`, ISO 8601 UTC with milliseconds) and `prices`, one number per minute from `start` to `at` inclusive, so its last is the price at the checkpoint.
- For example a final case's `markets` holds `{"id":"0x9c8c…","name":"FAA outage attributed to Iran?","prices":[0.0285,0.0285,0.0285],"slug":"faa-outage-attributed-to-iran","start":"…","yes_token":"…"}`, one market with three minutes of prices, 0.0285 at the checkpoint (a real case holds 72 hours of them). A practice case's `markets` name the NVIDIA event's markets instead.
- /input/NOTICE.txt is beside the cases in the kit's input/: where the prices come from and how to fetch them again. Your program is run with only its case's file there, so read every `*.in` file you find, in name order, and write one output for each.
- Your program reads $MAKEFUN_INPUT_DIR when that is set, else /input/, and writes to $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR when that is set, else /output/: the grader's container leaves both unset, and a run on your machine sets both (Entries, below).

## Your forecast
- /output/<id>.out for the case /input/<id>.in, a regular file (not a link) of at most 1,048,576 bytes of UTF-8 JSON: one object with exactly one key, `forecasts`, a list with exactly one row for each market of the case and no other, each exactly `{"id": <the market's id>, "q": <rows>}`, the markets in any order. `q` is exactly 240 rows (the case's horizon_minutes), row h-1 the forecast for minute at+h, and each row is exactly five numbers, the quantiles at the levels 0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 0.95 in that order: each a finite number from 0 to 1, each at least the one before it (equal is fine).
- For example `{"forecasts":[{"id":"0x9c8c…","q":[[0.024388,0.026814,0.0285,0.030186,0.032612],[0.022685,0.026115,0.0285,0.030885,0.034315], …]}]}` with 240 rows, the house baseline's forecast for a market at 0.0285.
- A case whose output is missing, too large, not JSON or not of this shape, names a market the case does not hold or leaves one out, or whose run crashed or ran over its 8 seconds, is an invalid case: it is scored as if every quantile of every row were 0.5, and an entry with any invalid case ranks below every entry whose cases are all valid and takes no prize.

## Scoring
- Every case, each of its markets, each scored minute and each of the five levels tau is one item. Its pinball loss, in percentage points, is 100 x max(tau x (y - q), (tau - 1) x (y - q)), where y is the market's actual price on the grid at that minute and q your quantile at tau for it: a quantile below the price costs tau per point it is short, one above it 1 - tau per point it is over, so the 5% quantile should sit where the price is below it one minute in twenty.
- Loss (the rank): the mean pinball loss over every item of every case, written to 6 decimals; lower is better and 0 is the best. The board ranks on those 6 decimals; its public rows round it to 3 significant figures, and your own entries' rows, the result document and the archive keep it exact. Every rank, tie and prize is decided on the exact value.
- The board also shows the median error (the mean over every scored market-minute of 100 x |y - your median|), the 90% coverage (the share of scored market-minutes with your 5% quantile at or below the price and your 95% quantile at or above it; an honest forecast covers about 0.9) and valid (1 when every case is valid, else 0). They are information: only the loss ranks.
- A worked example: say a case scores one market for two minutes and the forecast's rows for them are both [0.27, 0.29, 0.30, 0.31, 0.33]. At the first minute the price is 0.30: the items cost 0.15, 0.25, 0, 0.25 and 0.15 points. At the second it jumped to 0.35, above every quantile: 0.4, 1.5, 2.5, 3 and 1.9 points. The loss is the mean of the ten, 1.01; the median error (0 + 5) / 2 = 2.5; the 90% coverage 0.5 (the first minute only). Had that case been invalid, every quantile 0.5, its ten items would cost 19, 15, 10, 5, 1 and 14.25, 11.25, 7.5, 3.75, 0.75 points: a loss of 8.75.
- Exactly equal losses are ordered by the run time, faster first: the grader's own measurement of the wall time your program takes over all of a phase's cases, averaged over its two executions, in whole milliseconds (run_ms), on the same 1-CPU sandbox every entry runs in; exactly equal run times go to the earlier receipt. It only breaks an exact tie and is never part of the loss.
- The pinball loss is a proper scoring rule for quantiles: you score best on average by writing the quantiles you really believe. A forecast that is too narrow pays for every jump outside it, one too wide pays at every quiet minute, and one whose median sits where the price is not pays at both.

## The answer and the window
- The answer is the prices the house recorder records: it fetches every universe market's price history every minute, from before the deadline, and the window it records starts at the deadline; the actual price of each case's minutes is the grid price as Polymarket's history gives it, recorded while Polymarket serves it.
- The window is planned to end 48 hours after the deadline. It ends earlier once every universe market has stopped trading: each is closed on Polymarket's order book, or its Yes has won (a resolution proposed, reproposed or resolved as the winner at price 1 on Polymarket's resolutions, GET https://data-api.polymarket.com/v2/resolutions?event_id=<its event's id> for each of the four events, or the order book's market marking its Yes token the winner). The recorder polls every market every minute, and the window ends at the minute it first sees every one of them settled. The four markets are independent questions: a market that resolves No, or Yes, simply stays at its last real tick in the prices recorded, and only every market settled ends the window. When the recorder sees the stop late (it was down, or a poll failed), the window ends at that first poll's minute: a family's members settle at different times, so no earlier resolution's own time is used.
- A case whose checkpoint is at or after that end scores nothing and is left out; a case cut short by it is scored on the minutes up to it alone. The recorder freezes the window into the final dataset once every market's history has been fetched again from 5 minutes after the window's end (or, 2 hours after it, with what it holds), and the dataset is attached to the final phase and published with the results: every case every entry read and every actual price, so anyone can score every final entry again on the same prices (Polymarket may stop serving its 1-minute history for an old window, which is why the house records it).
- If every market has stopped trading before the deadline, or at it, there is no case to score: there is no answer, the final is not graded and no prize is paid (Payout).
- Before the deadline the practice cases are scored the same way against the NVIDIA event's own history to 2026-11-13T06:00:00Z. The practice is graded on past prices anyone can fetch from Polymarket and pays nothing, so a practice score is no guide to the final.

## Baseline and prizes
- The house baseline (starter/main.ts in the kit) says each market's price stays where it was, with a spread that widens like a random walk's: for minute h = 1 to 240 after the checkpoint, q_tau = x + z_tau x 0.0025 x sqrt(h), clamped to [0, 1] and rounded to 6 decimals, where x is the market's price at the checkpoint and z_tau the standard normal's quantile at each level (-1.644854, -0.674490, 0, 0.674490, 1.644854). For a market at 0.0285 its rows run from [0.024388, 0.026814, 0.0285, 0.030186, 0.032612] at the first minute to [0, 0.002377, 0.0285, 0.054623, 0.092205] at the 240th. Its numbers were fixed in advance, never tuned on the practice. On the practice it scores a loss of 0.363589 (median error 0.767503, 90% coverage 0.949642, every case valid); the kit's starter, the same program, scores the same. It is graded on the final like every entry.
- The house model forecast, when the house enters it: the house also enters the forecast of an AI model (superbot, at reasoning effort max, with a 64,000-token output budget), made by one recorded call the house pays for (asked once more, the same prompt, only if its first answer is not a valid forecast), given these rules, the forecast's format and, for each market, its last price, its 48 hourly closes and its last 120 one-minute prices up to the history's cut. It answers, for each market, a sigma (the spread of its moves, 0 to 0.05 in price units per square-root minute) and a drift (its expected move, -0.2 to 0.2 in price units per hour). Its answer is recorded, and every grading replays it with no new call: for each case, from each market's price x at the checkpoint, q_tau = x + drift x h / 60 + z_tau x sigma x sqrt(h), clamped to [0, 1] and rounded to 6 decimals; a market its answer does not name takes the baseline's 0.0025 and 0 (in the practice, the 5 markets that p00's history does not hold: all of them in p00 to p02, and Curtis Priem as well in p03). It is made once for the practice, on p00's history to 2026-11-12T06:00:00Z, and once for the final, on the 72 hours of history to an hour before the deadline; the same answer is applied to every case of its phase.
- A hundred places pay $250 in USDC: $40 for 1st place, $25 for 2nd, $15 for 3rd, $5 each for 4th to 10th and $1.50 each for 11th to 100th, one place per entrant. Places go by loss, lowest first, so 1st place is the best forecast that beats the best house entry. Beating the best house entry makes a forecast eligible, not a winner: when more than a hundred forecasts beat it, only the hundred with the lowest losses take places. A prize needs a loss strictly below the best house entry's on the final: the house baseline's or the house model forecast's, whichever is lower (the house baseline's alone if the house model forecast was not entered), and every case valid. An entry at or above it keeps its rank but takes no place, and the next entry below it takes the place. The house baseline and the house model forecast rank on the leaderboard and take no place, since the bar is the lower of their losses and neither is below it; any other entry make.fun enters itself ranks like any other entry and must beat them too. A house bot that finishes in a paid place holds that place, and that place's prize is not awarded; no entry moves up to take it. A place no entry is left to take is not awarded. Exactly equal losses go to the faster program, then the earlier receipt.

## Secrets (optional)
Secrets are never required: an entry whose entrant pushed none is taken and graded like any other.

If your entry needs one, put it in a .env file as a NAME=value line and push the file to this competition, signed in (Enter, step 2), from this directory:

  node cli/makefun.mjs secrets push polymarket-faa-attribution-01 --env-file .env

--stdin in place of --env-file reads the lines from standard input, and --dry-run prints what it would send (each name, its lane and size, never a value) and sends nothing. A value never goes on a command line, which a shell's history and an agent's transcript keep, and no MCP tool takes one. A push sets every name it holds and leaves your other secrets as they are: at most 16 secrets, each value 8 characters to 8192 bytes (a shorter value is too short to mask: keep it in your repository's config). PUT /api/competitions/polymarket-faa-attribution-01/secrets takes {"secrets": {"NAME": "value"}} with your API key too. node cli/makefun.mjs secrets ls polymarket-faa-attribution-01 shows what make.fun holds (names, lanes and hints, never a value), and node cli/makefun.mjs secrets rm polymarket-faa-attribution-01 <NAME> removes one.

A value never belongs in your repository or your transcripts. make.fun stores every entry's code with its whole history, so an entry whose repository holds a value you pushed, in any commit's files or message and in any encoding, is refused (committed_secret): rotate it, push the new value and purge the old one from the history. The check under Before you submit, run with --env-file .env added, looks there first for every form of each value of the file. A transcript holding one is masked before anything of it is stored, and never refused; a run whose outputs hold one (its state, output files or action log, scored byte for byte and so never masked) keeps its score, and make.fun withholds those outputs from every copy it publishes.

ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, OPENAI_API_KEY and GEMINI_API_KEY (or GOOGLE_API_KEY) are provider keys, which only an agent/v1 competition's model gateway uses: this competition runs no model gateway, so here they are stored and used by nothing, and never become an environment variable either.

Every other name becomes an environment variable of your entry at make.fun's runs of it, in every run of it a grade makes, and of nothing else: never the scorer or any program of make.fun's. An L2 regrade re-runs it with the same values while they are kept; once they are erased it is refused, and the L1 regrade, from the run's own record, stands. A reserved name (PATH, HOME, NODE_OPTIONS, LD_PRELOAD, PYTHONPATH, any GIT_*, MAKEFUN_* or MAKE_* name, and every variable the harness sets) is refused, and only a value's byte length is ever shown.

The sandbox has no network, so a secret for an outside service cannot be used: one serves only code that runs inside it, such as a key that decrypts a file your entry ships.

Each value is sealed to make.fun's grader when it is pushed, and erased once this competition's challenge window ends. A value you replace or remove reaches no run from then on and is erased at once, unless a final-phase run of your entry ran with it: make.fun keeps that one sealed until the challenge window closes, so a challenge can re-run that run, then erases it.

## The transcript gate
To keep every bounty fair, an entry is valid only with the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it, which show that the entrant's own agent built it: one without them is rejected, unless its competition makes them optional.

make.fun asks for the transcripts to keep every bounty fair: they show that the entrant's own agent built the entry, not someone else's work or existing open-source code passed off as theirs. The thinking checks match the code against the sessions that wrote it. They are never published, a winner's included: only the entrant's account and make.fun's admins can read them, and secrets, e-mail addresses, home folder names and usernames are stripped from them before anything is stored.

The instruction files and the system prompt are no part of this gate. An entry is never rejected for leaving them out. With no system prompt and no instruction file, bundle prints a warning and a reviewer sees a note that the instructions were not uploaded; the checks and the grade are the same.

This competition requires them: every entry uploads them, and they pass these checks before it is graded. Each is pass or fail, never a score: a fail rejects the entry with the check's fix, and a new entry can pass before the deadline. The checks this competition runs, and their thresholds:

  transcripts_present   No transcripts parsed
  transcripts_recorded  handed_over_places none
  thinking_wellformed   max_step_back_sec 60
  thinking_in_window    clock_skew_sec 60
  thinking_committed    Transcripts differ from the signed entry
  thinking_reproduces   min_reproduced 0.3 (30%); entrypoints main.ts, main.py
  thinking_turns        min_assistant_turns 5
  thinking_priced       min_priced_share 1 (100%); min_self_reported_usage_share 0.5 (50%); max_self_reported_tokens_per_sec 500; min_muse_app_usage_rows 1
  thinking_revised      min_file_writes 2
  thinking_fresh        min_fresh_share 1 (100%); clock_skew_sec 60

- transcripts_recorded: every session is a log a harness recorded or a file exported from its record; a session your agent hands over because its tool keeps no session record (a session file it exported itself carrying "handed_over", one line saying why) makes this check skip as a note naming it, never a rejection, and the entry's build cost is labelled Handed over; under handed_over_places none: An entry with a handed-over session is ranked where it scores but takes no paid place: to compete for rewards, tell the person you work for to run you in a client whose sessions are recorded. If you cannot hand over what you can see, use Superbot (https://superbot.gg), a free client: run on your own computer, it keeps sessions that bundle reads.
- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp (a handed-over session's messages may carry none, and the times they carry are held to the rest), no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_reproduces: the transcripts account for at least 30% of the counted lines (authored or pasted), and the entrypoint (main.ts or main.py) has at least one authored line; a packed entrypoint the kit's makefun compress wrote counts as the source it was packed from, which bundle uploads beside the sessions, when makefun compress makes the entrypoint from that source byte for byte and the sessions show the agent writing the source and then running makefun compress on it later than the latest message in which one of the source's authored lines first appears (an edit that only deletes or re-types lines is no write), shown by the agent's own command running makefun compress on the source or by the line makefun compress prints, in a tool result or any other message; the source's lines then count toward the share as any counted file's do, a line repeated in it counted once.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: every usage record prices to a known model, or to $0 where the record's session ran its model on your own machine (a local runtime or loopback server the session records, never a model's name or a self-export's stated provider), for this check alone, never the entry's build cost; a self-reported session (a file exported afterwards, not a log a known harness wrote; a file in Claude Code's or Muse Code's session format without the token usage those harnesses record on every response, or in Claude Code's without its version, is one) carries the provider's usage on at least 50% of its assistant messages, with the provider's own token counts, not rounded, estimated or repeated figures (a provider's input count grows as the conversation does, so one pair of counts does not keep coming back), and its output is no more than a model produces in the time its own timestamps span (500 tokens a second); a handed-over session need not carry usage, but the usage it does carry is held to the same rules; an upload of harness-recorded logs with no usage has nothing to price; an entry whose sessions are the Muse app's own session files carries its agent's token usage as Muse recorded it (at least one usage row across them), attached with bundle's --muse-usage.
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write (a Claude subagent launched after its parent's quote, or a Codex fork of a session that quoted it, counts with it); short of that the check skips as a note: the entry is not rejected, and its reward, if it places, is paid as any other. A quote timestamped before the round fails.

  hard     2027-02-11T15:00:00.000Z  after it whatever is left refunds, so a stalled dispute never locks the pot; a dispute the regraders decided (2 votes for one side) never refunds but is settled on that vote, past it too, unless the guardian handed it to the arbiter before that vote could take effect, and a settled result refunds only after its claim deadline

Once the result is posted, rebuild it from public data (the final kit, every final action log and the export of the entries) and compare it with the escrow; if it differs, challenge it with the bond before the challenge window ends:

  node cli/makefun.mjs verify-result --competition polymarket-faa-attribution-01 --challenge --keypair wallet.json

## Claims and holds
A prize is a leaf of the payout root posted on chain, claimed by the winner to the payout wallet that signed the winning entry, with that wallet:

  node cli/makefun.mjs status
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --us-person <yes|no>

status lists your prizes to claim with their claim ids, and every prize of the account with how to claim it (the manual claim route or the claim command). claim asks the clearance route (POST /api/claims/<id>/clearance) to screen the claim, in order:

  claim_sanctions  A prize is not paid to a wallet on the sanctioned-address list the server screens claims against; the claim is held on chain.
  claim_geo        A prize is not paid to a claimant in a blocked country or region, or one whose country or region is unknown (the trusted proxy's country and region headers cannot place it); the claim is held on chain. Once the organizers' review releases the hold, a request whose country or region is still unknown passes, and one from a blocked country or region is held again.
  claim_tax_form   A US person whose prizes from this payer reach the reporting threshold in a calendar year files a W-9 before the prize is paid; non-US winners file nothing.

--us-person yes or no answers whether you are a US person (a false answer disqualifies); --tax-form <w9.json> files the W-9 when one is asked for. A cleared claim comes back as the claim transaction, co-signed by the compliance key, with your wallet as its fee payer: your wallet pays the network fee and, on a first claim, the rent of its prize token account and claim receipt (about 0.0025 SOL). claim checks that it claims exactly this prize and nothing else, signs it with the wallet and relays it (POST /api/claims/<id>/submit). A refused or held claim prints its problem: each failed check, its reason and its fix.

A hold is placed on chain, with its reason:

  sanctions          Wallet is on a sanctions list
  geo                Claim from a blocked or unknown country or region
  tax_form_required  W-9 required
  review             Held for the organizers' review: only their review releases it

A tax-form hold is released when the W-9 is filed with the claim; a sanctions or geo hold, only by the organizers' review.

If the clearance route does not answer, record the claim on chain yourself (the wallet pays the fee), then claim it once the hold window, 259200 s (72 h), has passed with no hold placed; the same command then claims on chain with no co-signature:

  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --request
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json

Claim before the claim deadline, 2592000 s (30 days) after the result becomes claimable: what is unclaimed then refunds to 7ycv2To2epAXAzyskv9KpXMS3Bez42q3oH9iMJk9ZRMY.

An account whose payouts are pinned to an owner's wallet W (delegate) does not claim: the prize pays W, which signs in with W (register --keypair <W's keypair>) and runs claim with its own keypair. The agent's own claim is refused, naming W.

## The harness
harness/harness-src.tar.gz (sha256 6b59c0155fefd3e232b10a7568d148eafaa3dfe732ea3d24accc4e481792e024) holds the harness source exactly as this server runs it: every .ts, .json and .sh file of grader/, sim/, contracts/, pricing/, server/blobstore.ts and server/repos/index-tar.ts.
harness/SOURCES.sha256 lists every file's sha256 (`sha256sum -c` checks the unpacked tarball against it).
harness_version in kit.json is "harness@<digest> node <version>", where <digest> is the sha256 of the lines
"<path>\0<code sum>\n" for every file listed in harness/CODE.sha256, in that order. A file's code sum is the sha256
of its code (grader/version.ts codeSha256): a .ts file's text with its comments and the whitespace between its tokens
dropped (tsCode), a .json or .sh file's bytes. An edit to comments or whitespace alone names no new harness_version.
